# rp-core-model: the loss pipeline

**Where it goes.** In `concepts/core-model` (convert it to `.mdx`), right after the second paragraph of **Setup** ("Three families of quantities characterize π …"), before "## 1. Epistemic weight". It works as a map of the chapter: §1 (LR), §2 (losses + DPI), §3 (costs), §4 (corruption) and §5 (pricing) each sit at the point where they act. If the page should open with prose, the fallback spot is the end of "### The exact skeleton underneath", since that paragraph makes the figure's central claim ("bounds only loss, never bias").

**What it shows.**
- A band whose width is I(World; stage). It narrows across evaluator (L_eval), format (L_cod) and consumers (L_interp). A dotted line marks what the evidence held, so each loss shows as a gap. The bottom row reads the DPI: Evidence ≥ Report ≥ Interpretation.
- The mirror below the LR = 1 line is a captured evaluator. It has the same width, meaning the same bits, but the opposite sign. A judge who keeps the old LR is moved away from the truth. This is the one accent element (corruption), entered by "Adversary, capability g, pays C_π(Δ, g, t)".
- Costs: process π costs c_process once, and each of n consumers pays c_interp.
- Pricing: a dashed arrow from judge J back to π, labelled with the §5 identity.

**Data source.** `compute.py` builds a toy Markov chain and generates both SVGs. The chain is: H with prior 0.5 → 4-grade evidence → noisy evaluator → pass/fail rubric → consumer who skips with probability 0.3. Band widths are the exact mutual informations: 0.590, 0.481, 0.378 and 0.264 bits. These give L_eval 0.19, L_cod 0.21 and L_interp 0.30. The script asserts the following:
- The DPI holds.
- The page's product formula telescopes exactly when the losses are defined as MI ratios.
- Inverting the evaluator leaves every MI unchanged.
- The honest judge's expected log-score gain equals the MI.
- A stale-LR judge facing the captured process has negative value (−0.92 bits against +0.26).
- A judge who reads the inverted reports correctly recovers +0.26.
- The §5 identity holds on this process (0.0833 = 0.0833).

The parameters are illustrative because the page gives no numbers. The caption says so.

**Integration.** `figure.html` is a `<figure>` with two SVGs, a wide 680px one and a narrow 343px one. A container query (<600px) switches between them. Colours are CSS custom properties keyed on `[data-theme]` plus `prefers-color-scheme`. It needs no JS. Text is 15px and 14px, with subscripts at 12px and 11px.

**Page-text tensions.**
1. The page lists the losses as cod, eval, interp. The figure orders them evaluator → format, following the page's own example ("a rubric cannot carry what the evaluator saw"). The product is order-free, but a sentence saying where codification sits would help.
2. The page's chain is World → Evidence → Report → Interpretation. The figure adds an evaluator-verdict point inside π, which is a finer chain where the DPI still holds. The heuristic's L_eval and L_cod both fall inside the page's Evidence→Report step, and the page doesn't say so.
3. The vertical sign axis (above/below LR = 1) is schematic. Width is bits, and side is whether the judge's reading points toward or away from the truth. The page's "little or reversed weight" covers the partial case. The figure draws only the extreme case, which the page explicitly names ("destroy no information while still pointing the judge the wrong way").
4. In the toy, the stale-LR judge loses 0.92 bits of expected log score, about 3.5× the 0.26 bits the honest process delivers. The figure and caption now show both numbers: "negative value" in failure mode 3 isn't bounded by the positive value. The ratio is specific to this toy, but the asymmetry is the general point, and the page could say so in one sentence.
