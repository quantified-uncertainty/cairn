Evaluation Engineering

Evaluation engineering is the design of systems that produce large numbers of estimates and evaluations, such as grant reviews, charity assessments and research triage, at known cost and under incentives that keep them honest.

We know how to produce one careful evaluation of a grant or a charity. The bet here is that the hard part is now the ten-thousandth: producing it cheaply and reliably enough that the results get used. The core pattern is old, with precedents well outside this project. A cheap layer scores every item, a hidden random sample also goes to an expensive, trusted evaluator, and that sample measures how far the cheap layer can be trusted.

Who pays is part of the design. In the wiki’s coding of about 160 standing systems, those paid by the parties they rate had about 3.5 times the odds of a documented failure.

This is not the AI-model “evals” field, though the two overlap wherever an LLM is the judge. The pages are working notes, not settled positions. They were drafted largely with Claude Code, and each states its own status.

Start with Chapter 1, or see the ideas applied in the worked example: a funder reviewing 1,000 grants a year under four designs, with illustrative costs.
