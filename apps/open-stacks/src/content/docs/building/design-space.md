Prototype descriptions follow the source files, build data and About panels as of 2026-10-15, when the Epoch Slider and Front Table pages were not yet in the repository (their rows describe the spec plus the build data). ## The short version

- **Evidence already fixes four defaults.** Part III supports a ranked list beside any overview, 2D or 2.5D layouts that stay put, source text shown before generated text, and a page that checks what the model claims. The open choices are the unit of navigation, how far the reader's own epistemics enter the interface, and whether anyone else is in it.
- **Every concept shares one pattern: the model selects, the page verifies.** The model points at lines, records and numbers. Deterministic code resolves the pointers, matches quotations verbatim, recomputes arithmetic and rejects identifiers not in the catalog. The build scripts verified all 164 Crux Table quotations, all 348 Chapter Lenses pointers and all 258 Gearbox quotations and anchors. Verification proves a quotation exists, not that the stance attached to it is right. If you copy one thing, copy this pattern, plus a precomputed fallback for every generated feature and an access label on every item [I]; the concepts themselves are untested with readers.
- **From 49 concepts to 18 to 12.** Seven persona agents proposed 49 concepts; merging folded 48 into 18 specified candidates. Three judges scored usefulness, novelty and demo-ability; totals ran from 13 to 20.
- **Novelty traded against usefulness.** Across the 18, novelty correlated negatively with usefulness (r = −0.46) and with demo-ability (r = −0.56). The twelve built concepts averaged 5.2 for usefulness and 5.9 for novelty; the six unbuilt ones 4.0 and 6.8.
- **The build list is not the top twelve by score** [I]. Two overlapping concepts were folded into other builds. Three lower scorers were built to cover regions nothing else covered: Scatter/Gather, the spatial room, and time. Of the four skipped outright, one needs weeks of use to show its value, one needs lecture transcripts and video, one needs legible 3D, and one works better as an analysis than as an app.
- **The empty regions are social reading, chapter-level units for in-copyright books, and measurement.** Build next, in order: a page-level "chapter shelf" from HTRC Extracted Features (HTRC ended its services on 2026-09-30, so mirror the files now); a Reading Portfolio as a personal tool; a search-first Sieve with a synced list; and MCP Apps versions of the Circulation Desk and Page Flags.
## Principles from Part III

These thirteen principles are distilled from [Foraging and Exploratory Search](/interfaces/exploratory-search/), [Maps of Knowledge](/interfaces/maps-of-knowledge/), [Reading Interfaces](/interfaces/reading-interfaces/), [Generative Interfaces](/interfaces/generative-interfaces/), [Building a Virtual Reading Room](/environments/virtual-reading-room/) and [Searching Inside Books You Don't Have](/finding/searching-inside-books/). Confidence is my credence that the principle holds for a book-and-paper explorer used by one researcher.

| # | Principle | Evidence | Confidence | Prototypes that apply it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Source first, generated gloss second, labeled** | Only 51.5% of generative-search sentences were fully supported by their citations ([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)). People who learned from LLM syntheses reported shallower knowledge, even with links appended (seven experiments, n = 10,462; [Melumad & Yun 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf316)). Gists *beside* the text made reading easier with similar comprehension (n = 24; [Paper Plain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00130)) | ~85% | Every prototype labels AI text (a project rule); Crux Table and Page Flags render the source's words first |
| 2 | **The model selects; the page quotes, resolves and recomputes** | In grounded systems the main error is misattribution, not fabrication: citation accuracy of 40–80% across deep-research systems ([DeepTRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04499)); about 15% of sentences unsupported by their citations ([STORM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207)). A valid pointer proves the span exists, not that it supports the claim | ~85% | Crux Table, Chapter Lenses, Gearbox, Evidence Ledger, Bindery, Page Flags |
| 3 | **No reference reaches the screen without catalog resolution** | Of 18 checked citations in a 2025 Springer Nature machine-learning book, about two-thirds did not exist or had substantial errors ([Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/30/springer-nature-book-on-machine-learning-is-full-of-made-up-citations/)) | ~90% | Bindery's resolver gate; Circulation Desk lets the model pick only records a local search returned; Terra Incognita's field guides |
| 4 | **Generate data into a fixed kit of components, not free-form pages** | Generated pages beat markdown answers in 82.8% of comparisons, but raters judged preference, not accuracy, and generation "can often take a minute or two" ([Leviathan et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09577)). The standards converged on templates plus data ([MCP Apps](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-01-26-mcp-apps/), [A2UI](https://github.com/google/A2UI)) | ~85% | Chapter Lenses (a ten-component typed kit) |
| 5 | **Curate what carries bibliographic or legal weight, precompute what many readers see, generate live only what is personal, and give every live feature a fallback** | A FABLES-style human check cost about \$40 per book summary ([Kim et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)), the price of about 200 Sonnet 5.5 or 4,000 Haiku 5.5 batch gist ladders at October 2026 prices ([Reading Interfaces](/interfaces/reading-interfaces/)). An English Wikipedia content guideline, adopted after a March 2026 RfC, prohibits using LLMs to generate or rewrite article content ([WP:LLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:LLM)) | ~85% | Every prototype in the repository ships precomputed output and runs without a model on the static wiki |
| 6 | **Search first, then structure the results, with a list beside any structure** | Scatter/Gather taught a collection's structure but, without search, found fewer relevant documents than similarity search ([Pirolli et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1145/238386.238489)). The combined effect of visualization on retrieval was not significant in a meta-analysis (Chen & Yu 2000, via [Hearst ch. 10](http://searchuserinterfaces.com/book/sui_ch10_visualization.html)) | ~80% | Page Flags and Crux Table start from a query. Sieve breaks the rule: it has no search box |
| 7 | **Labels are the lever: few clusters, siblings labeled together, stated criteria** | LLM names matched 88% of expert subtopics on one biomedical benchmark ([Knowledge Navigator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15836)). Users rated 20, and especially 40, result groups "too many" (Käki 2005, n = 27, via [Hearst ch. 8](http://searchuserinterfaces.com/book/sui_ch8_navigation_and_search.html)) | ~80% | Sieve (nine piles named in one batched call) |
| 8 | **2D or 2.5D, a stable layout, and an overview beside undistorted detail** | 19 of 20 readers preferred overview+detail to a fisheye (Hornbæk & Frøkjær, via [Hearst ch. 10](http://searchuserinterfaces.com/book/sui_ch10_visualization.html)). Retrieval took 3.7 s in 2D and 4.8 s in 3D (p = .09; [Cockburn & McKenzie 2002](https://www.csse.canterbury.ac.nz/~andy/papers/chi02DM.pdf)); "interactive 3D rarely helps but often hurts" ([Sedlmair et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2013.153)). Personal placement of about 100 items aided retrieval ([Data Mountain](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/data-mountain-using-spatial-memory-for-document-management/)) | ~85% | Round Reading Room (a 2.5D wall with a 2D unroll), Terra Incognita (a frozen layout), Gearbox (a fore-edge overview) |
| 9 | **Map position indexes regions, not similarity** | On a 20.7M-paper PubMed map, 6.2% of 2D nearest neighbors were true nearest neighbors ([González-Márquez et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2024.100968)) | ~80% | Terra Incognita and the Round Reading Room lay out by call number, not by an embedding projection |
| 10 | **Keep the path: history, basket and trail** | Bates's berrypicking moves and "temporary store" ([1989](https://pages.gseis.ucla.edu/faculty/bates/berrypicking.html)); Shneiderman's neglected *history* and *extract* tasks ([1996](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Shneiderman1996eyes.pdf)) | ~75% | Sieve's funnel log, which doubles as a back button; the Circulation Desk's "tonight packet" (a basket of links, holds and an interlibrary-loan list) |
| 11 | **Reading depths are different tasks with different costs** | Speed reading as sold does not work ([Rayner et al. 2016](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/so-much-to-read-so-little-time-how-do-we-read-and-can-speed-readi/)). Skimming costs comprehension (n = 60; [Strukelj & Niehorster](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198234/)) and beats reading half the text only for main ideas ([Duggan & Payne 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19751073/)). Practice testing is "high utility"; highlighting is "low" ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)) | ~80% | Gearbox: five gears with stated time costs, and a bet before each chapter (pretesting) |
| 12 | **Show access on every item** | The recency cliff: 82% of pre-1990 candidates in one worked example could be borrowed or page-searched, against 4 of 31 from 2010 on ([Searching Inside Books](/finding/searching-inside-books/)). In this wiki's 7,127-work sample, about 4% were free to read and 28% borrowable; across all of Open Library the borrowable share is nearer 7% ([Virtual Reading Room](/environments/virtual-reading-room/)) | ~80% that it belongs on every item; whether it changes what people read is untested | Page Flags, Sieve, Terra Incognita and the Round Reading Room mark every item by access; the Bindery and the Circulation Desk route them |
| 13 | **Treat preference as weak evidence; measure foraging and learning** | Users significantly preferred a 3D layout that gave no benefit ([Cockburn & McKenzie 2001](http://www.cosc.canterbury.ac.nz/andrew.cockburn/papers/chi01DM.pdf)). In 124 exploratory-search user studies with simulated work tasks (2010–2021), the median sample was 24 and five exceeded 100 ([Medlar et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13695)) | ~85% | None yet: no prototype logs outcomes |

Principles 1–5 come from the error evidence on generated content, 6–10 from thirty years of interface studies, 11 from reading research, and 13 from the state of the evidence itself. Principle 12 has the weakest outcome evidence and the strongest structural case: access is the attribute a reader acts on next, and most discovery tools rank without it [I]. If you adopt only four, take 2, 3, 5 and 12: they are cheap, the first three block the documented failures of generated content, and the fourth answers the question the reader asks next [I].

## The axes of the space

Any concept is a set of choices along six axes. The table gives the positions on each axis, what the evidence says about them, and the default I would pick.

| Axis | Positions | What the evidence says | Default [I] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit of navigation** | corpus · shelf (call-number range) · book · chapter · page · passage · claim | Overviews help newcomers learn a field's shape; lookup needs passages. The chapter is the natural unit for reading across disciplines, but the data is thinnest there: about 2.5% of Open Library editions carry a table of contents (R05's count of the September 2026 dump, which its verification pass could not reproduce; see [Catalogs and Metadata](/finding/catalogs-and-metadata/)) | Browse by shelf and book, choose by chapter, verify by passage |
| **What is generated** | curated · precomputed and spot-checked · generated live · never generated | Checking costs far more than generating. Labeling the clusters for one Scatter/Gather step costs about \$0.003 on Claude Haiku 5.5 or \$0.06 on Sonnet 5.5 ([Foraging and Exploratory Search](/interfaces/exploratory-search/)); a gist ladder for a book about \$0.01 on Haiku 5.5 batch (October 2026 prices; [Reading Interfaces](/interfaces/reading-interfaces/)) | Curate references, rights and quotations; precompute summaries and labels; generate live only what is personal |
| **Layout** | ranked list · facets · 2D map · 2.5D wall or shelf · 3D or VR | Lists win targeted retrieval. Facets have the best positive evidence (90–91% preference, n = 32; [Flamenco](https://flamenco.berkeley.edu/papers/flamenco-chi03.pdf)). 3D gave no measured benefit. Headsets add space, not legible text: about 20–37 pixels per degree against roughly 60 for 20/20 vision ([Building a Virtual Reading Room](/environments/virtual-reading-room/)) | List plus facets by default; a stable 2D or 2.5D overview; VR as one mode over the same data |
| **Readers** | one reader · a shared, reviewed layer · many readers interacting | Personal placement of about 100 items aids retrieval. Shared generated artifacts need review: Grokipedia's machine-only edit loop is the counterexample (see [Generative Interfaces](/interfaces/generative-interfaces/)) | Personal first; a shared layer only for reviewed artifacts |
| **Epistemic features** | none · provenance labels · verbatim verification · formal updating (likelihood ratios) · calibration scoring · coverage estimates | Provenance and verification follow from principles 1–3. Likelihood-ratio ledgers, calibration scoring and coverage estimates are untested as interfaces | Labels and verification always; scoring features opt-in |
| **Access-awareness** | none · access color · routing to a copy · cost optimization across a list | Legal free text covered 11 of 33 famous technical books and 24 of 55 canonical books, but about 4% of the 7,127-work catalog sample. The rest route to a library card, a loan, a subscription or a purchase ([The Access Ladder](/access/access-ladder/)) | Color on every item, a route on click, batch optimization for lists |

Two more axes cut across these. **Time**: most interfaces are synchronic, but the Epoch Slider and the Round Reading Room's height axis make publication year a control. **Push or pull**: the Front Table pushes a curated selection; every other concept answers a query.

Three patterns stand out.

- **Every candidate is single-reader** apart from the Front Table's shareable seeds. None of the seven lenses represented a community of readers, which probably explains the gap [~70%]. Two social concepts follow from the evidence in [Curation](/finding/curation/) [I]: a curator graph (whose public bookshelves and syllabi overlap with yours), since that chapter recommends curator- and syllabus-weighted signals as the primary filter; and a review-first queue (read the best review before the book). Karnofsky's own guess is about 25% understanding of a book for 2 hours of reviews and discussion, against 12–15% for skimming or one reading, and he later limited the advice to reading outside one's expertise.
- **Chapters are a popular unit that the data cannot yet supply.** Five specs choose the chapter (Gearbox, the Front Table, the Bindery, Chapter Lenses, the Reading Portfolio). In the builds, chapters exist only for public-domain texts and for in-copyright books with a fetched table of contents. For the Front Table's 92 curated works, Open Library had an English table of contents in some edition for 43.
- **No candidate trusts a model-typed quotation or reference** [I]. Every spec resolves ids or string-matches quotations in page code, in line with the "never generate" row of [Generative Interfaces](/interfaces/generative-interfaces/). The panel brief is not in the record, so whether the panel was told to do this is unknown.

## The ideation process

### Seven lenses, 49 concepts, 18 candidates

Seven personas each proposed seven concepts, each a name and a one-line pitch: an information-visualization researcher, a librarian and bookseller, a game designer, a generative-UI designer, an epistemics designer, a spatial designer and a reading scientist ([record](https://github.com/quantified-uncertainty/cairn/blob/main/apps/open-stacks/research/ideation-panel.json)). A curation pass merged overlapping concepts into 18 candidates, each with a full specification: core interaction, signature moment, screens, data needed, the model's role, prior art, risks, a feasibility rating (1–5) and a best form (light prototype or deep mockup). Only *Loci*, a memory palace built from each finished book's argument, was dropped.

Independent lenses converged. The infovis and reading lenses each independently proposed a "Syntopicon Live" (one question, every author's answer, each quotation linked to its paragraph), and the epistemics lens proposed the Crux Table; all three became one candidate. The infovis and reading lenses also both proposed "Synteny" (genome-style alignment of lectures and textbooks). The game and epistemics lenses each proposed a fog-of-war map. Convergence is weak evidence that an idea is good, but decent evidence that it follows naturally from the data on hand [I].

### Judging

Three judges each scored one criterion and recommended a form (light prototype, deep mockup or skip). As their notes apply them, *usefulness* means repeated use in this reader's real reading decisions, *novelty* means distance from known tools and research, and *demo-ability* means a convincing build in about a day on the shared datasets. The record does not state the scale; scores run from 2 to 9, and the total is their sum. The judges checked some claims against the data: for example, that Laplace's text multiplies 1/1000 by "6/10" and prints 9/10000 at line 2890, and that Search Inside answered from the build machine in about 5 seconds per query.

### What the scores say

- **Novelty and usefulness pulled apart.** Across the 18, r(usefulness, novelty) = −0.46, r(novelty, demo) = −0.56 and r(usefulness, demo) = 0.21. The most novel ideas (Epoch Slider at 9; the Great Conversation, the Round Reading Room and Synteny at 8) were among the least useful by the judges' lights, scoring 3, 3, 3 and 4. The most useful (the Circulation Desk at 8) was the least novel but one: its novelty judge called it "useful logistics, not a new way to explore literature". With n = 18 and one judge per criterion, judge and criterion are confounded, so part of this may be one judge's framing [~50% that the negative sign holds with human judges].
- **The usefulness judge was the harshest.** Mean scores were 4.8 for usefulness, 6.2 for novelty and 5.2 for demo-ability. Four candidates drew a "skip" from the usefulness judge: the Great Conversation, Epoch Slider, the Round Reading Room and Prophecy Audit.
- **The useful core is usually narrower than the concept.** Judges repeatedly said the value sat in one view. For Terra Incognita it was the coverage estimate ("The fog map, empire and Guess mode are mostly decoration"). For the Circulation Desk it was the routing board and export; for the Bindery, the brief plus the hours slider; for the Round Reading Room, the 2D unroll.

## Why these twelve

The selection followed four rules, reconstructed from the judges' notes and the builds [I]:

1. **Build the top of the table where a build is feasible.** Page Flags, the Evidence Ledger, the Circulation Desk and Gearbox scored at least 6 on usefulness and 6 on demo-ability, and all three judges recommended a light prototype.
2. **Fold overlaps into one build instead of dropping the idea.** The usefulness judge said the Great Conversation's "useful core duplicates the Crux Table". The Crux Table build has a Conversation view and page-detected echo arcs, the Great Conversation's best mechanisms. Core Sample's usefulness judge said it "overlaps heavily with the Bindery", and the Bindery build has a Route view that drills from a paper to the classics beneath it and solves for the cheapest way back up.
3. **Cover each region of the space at least once, even at lower scores.**
   - **Sieve** (13, the lowest total) is the direct test of Part III's claim that labels, not the interaction, sank Scatter/Gather. Its novelty judge said it was worth building "only as shared embedding and cluster infrastructure".
   - **The Round Reading Room** (14) is the single spatial and VR piece. It tests the Virtual Reading Room chapter's claims on real data, with a 2D unroll for comparison.
   - **The Epoch Slider** (15) had the panel's highest novelty score (9) and is the only concept organized around the time axis.
   - **Chapter Lenses** tests the declarative generative-UI pattern of principle 4.
   - **The Front Table** is the only push-curation concept.
4. **Skip what a one-day static demo cannot show.**

| Prophecy Audit | 14 | Usefulness 2: "it belongs as a wiki analysis, not an app" | Tetlock's *Expert Political Judgment*, Ascher's *Forecasting* (1978), [Kent's words of estimative probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_of_estimative_probability), published audits of futurists | Automated extraction and Brier-sorted shelves | Contested, hindsight-biased resolutions; small n per book; Edwardian hedges mapped onto a 1964 scale | Nowhere yet |

## The twelve as built: prior art and main risk

Builds departed from the specs, usually by cutting to the judges' "useful core", sometimes because the data could not support the spec. The departures are findings about the data. The [Prototype Gallery](/building/prototype-gallery/) has screenshots and links.

| Prototype | As built (October 2026) | Closest prior art | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Page Flags** | 59 precomputed concepts (the spec said about 500). Lane 1 scans nine Gutenberg texts live in a Web Worker. Lane 2 is a Search Inside snapshot of the top 60 English hits per concept. Exact-page flags exist only for open scans. Lending and print-disabled scans get one "sealed slip", because the API's `page_num` is the scan's length, not the page of the hit ([Searching Inside Books](/finding/searching-inside-books/)). The two-term overlay is built; packets are not | [TileBars](https://people.ischool.berkeley.edu/~hearst/research/tilebars.html) (1995), Seesoft (1992), HathiTrust full-text search, back-of-book indexes | Page locations for in-copyright books are missing: Search Inside gives none, and HathiTrust blocks scripts. Literal matching misses period vocabulary |
| **Evidence Ledger** | One proposition: LLM forecasting systems match superforecasters, resolving as of October 2026, default prior 40%. 235 arXiv abstracts screened, 22 admitted as rows, 28 set aside with reasons. Four departures recur, and each is a finding about the data [I]:

- **Page locations for in-copyright books are the binding gap.** The specs for Page Flags, Lights Out, the Great Conversation's sealed columns and the Crux Table's ghost columns all assumed Search Inside returns hit pages. It does not, and HathiTrust's page search refuses scripts.
- **Embeddings gave way to cheaper features.** Sieve shipped with TF-IDF and the Round Reading Room's whisper with subject headings. Neither has been compared with an embedding version, so what was lost is unknown.
- **Statistical machinery shrank to what the data supports.** Terra Incognita swapped capture–recapture across probes for a single-log richness estimate. The Evidence Ledger settled on one proposition.
- **Dating is real work.** Gutenberg's catalog gives the year a book was *added*, so the Round Reading Room and the Epoch Slider each needed a dating rule and an audit.

## What to build next

In order of expected value for this reader per week of building [I]:

1. **A page-level "chapter shelf" from HTRC Extracted Features.** This fills the binding gap above with open data. EF 2.5 holds per-page token counts for 18.7M volumes, 9.97M in copyright, under CC BY 4.0 ([EF 2.5](https://htrc.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/COM/pages/975306753/Extracted+Features+v.2.5)). This wiki's worked example went from 179 candidate books to printed page locations for 43 of them in about 15 minutes. HTRC discontinued its services on 2026-09-30 ([HTRC](https://analytics.hathitrust.org/)). The files were still downloadable on 2026-10-15, and I put ~60% on their still being available from an official host on 2027-06-30. **Mirror your subset now**: about 2.4–3.9 GB per 10,000 volumes. The main obstacle is the recency cliff, since 1 of 31 post-2010 candidates was in HathiTrust.
2. **The Reading Portfolio as a personal tool, not a demo.** Its spec calls it "the concept the reader would open every week", and it scored low on demo-ability only because its value needs weeks to appear. Build the frontier scatter (hours against expected shift), pre-registered "will this move me?" forecasts and a post-read log. After eight weeks, the calibration of predicted against realized shifts is the result, whichever way it comes out.
3. **A search-first Sieve.** Add a search box, a synced ranked list, papers and real embeddings: the version principles 6 and 7 support. Test it within one reader against a faceted list on relevant items found per minute.
4. **MCP Apps versions of the Circulation Desk and Page Flags.** Both are deterministic tools over a catalog and fit the "fixed component per tool" architecture. With `ui://` templates, one server renders in every MCP Apps host (at the January 2026 launch: Claude, Goose and VS Code Insiders, with ChatGPT rolling out).
5. **A social layer** [I]. Build a curator graph over experts' public shelves and syllabi, a review-first queue, and "generate, then promote": an artifact that several readers request enters a review queue before joining a shared layer ([Generative Interfaces](/interfaces/generative-interfaces/)). The obstacle is data: [Curation](/finding/curation/) found hand-curated lists deep but mostly old (Five Books' statistics interviews date from 2011), with the active curation in review communities.
6. **One computed Synteny alignment** for a pairing with a free book, such as Harvard's Stat 110 with Blitzstein and Hwang's free PDF, as the spec suggests. Check transcript availability and license before building.
7. **The Prophecy Audit as a wiki analysis**, not an app: three to five public-domain futurology books, with hand-checked resolutions and the Kent mapping shown.

**If you are building your own, start from the closest prototype:**

| If you want... | Start from | Build first | Main obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which books treat a concept, and on which pages | Page Flags | Search Inside for candidates; EF page counts for location; HathiTrust by hand to verify | EF hosting after HTRC closes; recent books |
| To triage a syllabus or a paper's references | Circulation Desk | Regex and fuzzy matching against your catalog; the model only for leftovers, picking among real records | Holdings data; dated routes |
| To read a literature as evidence on a question | Evidence Ledger | Claim extraction with verbatim spans; an LR ledger with blind mode on by default | LRs from abstracts are crude; anchoring |
| To decide which chapters of one book to read | Gearbox | Gists linked to spans, plus a fore-edge overview; bets later | Needs the full text |
| To orient in an unfamiliar field | Sieve, with search added | LLM-labeled clusters over search results, beside a list | Label consistency across levels |
| To compare authors on one question | Crux Table | TileBars, then a stance matrix with verified quotations | Misattributed stances |
| A syllabus sized to your hours | Bindery | A resolver gate and a deterministic hours allocator | Tables of contents for in-copyright books |
| A reading room in a headset | Round Reading Room | The 2D unroll first, then the same data in WebXR | Legibility; no measured benefit from 3D |

For every row: generation through Claude artifacts costs the builder nothing per use, since usage "counts against each person's own plan limits" (as of October 2026; see [Generative Interfaces](/interfaces/generative-interfaces/)). Build costs are in [Costs and Effort](/building/costs-and-effort/), and the shared catalog these tools read is specified in [A Reference Architecture](/building/reference-architecture/).

## Open questions

- **Does page-side verification change what readers believe?** Show one group Crux Table cells with verified quotations and another the same cells as model paraphrases, seed one wrong stance in each, and compare detection rates.
- **Does access color change what people read?** Log the share of opened items that are readable tonight, with color on and off, over two weeks per condition.
- **How many chapter-level units can be recovered for in-copyright books?** For a 500-book shelf, count the books with an Open Library table of contents, an EF running-head offset, or neither. The EF worked example recovered offsets for 43 of 49 volumes; the Front Table's TOC fetch found a table of contents for 43 of 92 popular works. Neither sample is random.
- **Do proposed likelihood ratios anchor readers?** Compare the correlation between readers' and the model's LRs with blind mode on and off in the Evidence Ledger.
- **Is Terra Incognita's Chao1 interval calibrated?** Hide a known list of relevant works in a region, run foraging sessions, and check how often the 80% interval covers the true count.
- **Does bet-first gear selection save time without losing comprehension?** Run a Duggan–Payne-style test on main ideas, details and inferences for chapters read at the chosen gear against chapters read in full.
- **Does the Round Reading Room's wall build region memory that its 2D unroll does not?** Test recall of where five subjects sit after a month of use in each.
- **Does year-capped retrieval stop leakage?** Have a blind reviewer count anachronistic ideas, not just words, in Epoch Slider answers at 1880 and 1920.
- **Would human judges rank the 18 the same way?** Compute the Spearman correlation between the AI judges' totals and rankings by five target readers, and check whether the negative novelty–usefulness correlation survives.
- **Would a community lens produce viable social concepts?** Rerun the panel with an eighth lens (a reading-group organizer or a review editor) and see whether any of its concepts survive judging.
