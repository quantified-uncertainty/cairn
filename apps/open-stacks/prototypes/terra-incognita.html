About this chart

A deep mockup for Open Stacks: the Library of Congress shelf order unrolled into a world, so that the shape of what you have not read becomes visible, plannable and estimable.

What it demonstrates

  • Shelf order as geography. LC classes are continents, subclasses are provinces, outline ranges are towns, works are settlements. Area is proportional to catalog works plus Gutenberg titles; the provinces run along a Gosper space-filling curve, so call-number neighbours are map neighbours.
  • Fog of war. Each work the reader has read clears its hex and the ring around it; the next ring is half-fogged and glows. Brighter frontier hexes hold more popular, more accessible unread works.
  • Expedition planner. Picks 5–8 unread works in a region and orders them from introductory to specialised, with access status for each.
  • Unseen species. Treats the reader's encounters with a region's works as a biological survey: species-accumulation curve, bias-corrected Chao1 with a log-normal interval, a Good–Turing stopping rule, and a check against what the catalog actually holds.
  • Field guides. Claude writes a guide to a province on request, calling a search tool over that province's records so every cited work resolves.

Real data

  • 7,127 Open Library works (CC0; Search API, fetched 2026-10-15): titles, call numbers, editions, reading-log counts, first sentences, access status. 6,518 have a call number; the 609 without one sit on the Uncharted Isles.
  • 29,007 Project Gutenberg nonfiction titles (catalog feed, fetched 2026-10-15), counted by LoC class and drawn as forest ("hinterland").
  • Library of Congress Classification Outline: class, subclass and range names.
  • The catalog was assembled from 50 subject searches, so it is a 7k sample with artificial coastlines, heavy in science, social science and philosophy. Some Open Library years are wrong (first-publication dates are crowd-edited).

Precomputed and AI-generated

  • The layout, fog, frontier scores, routes and estimates are computed in your browser from the data (layout script: scripts/prototypes/terra-incognita.mjs).
  • Six field guides (QA, Q, HB, BF, BD, BC) were written by Claude in October 2026 from these records; they are AI-generated, every cited id was checked against the catalog, and they should be spot-checked before you rely on them.

Mocked

  • The example reader is synthetic: 22 hand-picked books plus a seeded sample of 128 more from probability, decision theory, machine learning, economics and philosophy of science, with invented dates and an invented search history. Paste your own list under Explorer… (it stays in this browser).
  • The encounter log behind Unseen species is derived, not observed: it is what this app would have shown that reader (shelf neighbours of each book read, subject cross-references, and the top results of each search).
