    About this prototype

    The Circulation Desk is a light prototype of batch access triage. Paste a reading list and every line is matched to a real catalog record, then routed to the cheapest legal way to read it tonight: a free copy, an Internet Archive loan, a library ebook, a subscription, or a used copy. It totals the cost and reading hours and names the single key (a library card or a subscription) that would open the most of what is left.

    What is real

    • The 28-book registry behind the sample list. Free copies come from the author-hosted table and the 55-book coverage test in the Open Stacks research dossier R01. O'Reilly and Perlego presence, Libby holdings at nine library systems, used prices (AbeBooks) and every subscription price come from dossier R03. Both were checked on 15 October 2026.
    • English loan checks. For each book R01 lists as borrowable, the build script read Internet Archive item metadata to find an English scan in the lending collection. That is how the board knows that Thinking, Fast and Slow lends only in translation.
    • Matching. Lines are matched against the registry, 7,127 Open Library works (catalog.json, CC0), 84 OpenStax textbooks and, on request, 29,007 Project Gutenberg titles. Books outside the registry get routes from their catalog fields (open scan, loan flag, print-disabled flag, Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks ids).
    • Link checks. Every free-copy URL was fetched when the data was built; the status is shown on the route.

    What is estimated or illustrative

    • Library keys use R03's Bay Area sample (SFPL, five other Bay Area systems, LAPL) as a worked example. Your own library's holdings will differ.
    • Subscription coverage for titles nobody checked is estimated from publisher shares (rules O2, E2). Everand's catalog could not be checked, and Kindle Unlimited routes nothing.
    • Hold waits assume two weeks per loan cycle. Reading hours use Open Library's median page count at assumed paces (textbook 10 pages an hour, monograph 25, popular 45).
    • Nothing is re-checked live: the page cannot reach other sites. Treat every route as a dated snapshot.

    What Claude does

    • Precomputed (AI-generated): explanations for six books and the resolutions of the two messy lines in the sample, written by Claude in October 2026 and labelled in place.
    • Live, when opened in Claude: "Ask Claude" resolves an unmatched line. It can only pick a record returned by a local catalog search, or name a work as unverified, which the desk then routes by rule. "Explain with Claude" writes a short rationale that must cite rule codes; codes that do not exist are struck through. Routing, totals and the advisor stay deterministic.

    Never

    No shadow libraries, ever (rule X1). Print-disabled scans are offered only if you say you are eligible.
