The Table

Rows are sub-questions, columns are books in order of first publication. Each mark places an author between the row's two poles; the shaded strip under it shows where in that book the question is discussed. Silence is a finding too: the strip shows how little the book says.

The Crux

Pick two authors. The table collapses to the rows where they actually diverge, ordered so that the disagreement that would explain the most of the others comes first.

    About this prototype

    A deep mockup for Open Stacks: Adler's Syntopicon rebuilt for one shelf and one question, with every quotation checked against the source by the page itself. The table, conversation, crux and vocabulary views are fully interactive on precomputed data; the "Add a Question" view generates a new row live when the page is opened inside Claude.

    What it demonstrates

    • Model selects, page quotes. The language model proposes stances and passages; a deterministic step looks each quotation up in the Gutenberg text and shows the source's words, never the model's. A quotation that does not match is dropped (build time) or struck out (live).
    • Where the question lives. TileBars (Hearst, CHI 1995) show, with no model at all, which stretches of each book discuss each sub-question. Silent cells keep their strip, so silence is visible evidence.
    • Reuse the model cannot fake. The page detects shared 10-word runs and rare numbers across books ("echoes"), e.g. Venn reprinting Laplace's 1,826,214 and Jevons's pilot fish. Echo arcs are solid; the model's "responds to" links are dashed.
    • The crux. For any pair of authors the table collapses to divergent rows, ordered by an (AI-proposed) dependency graph between sub-questions, and flags verbal disputes about the word "probability".

      What is mocked or limited

      • Live generation needs the Claude artifact runtime (sample); on the static wiki the "Add" view shows a precomputed example row, labelled as such, plus a no-AI passage finder that works anywhere.
      • Locators are Gutenberg line numbers plus chapter, section (Venn's §, Hume's Selby-Bigge ¶) or essay; these texts carry no printed page numbers.
      • Dates are first publication; the wording quoted is that of the edition named for each book (Hume's 1777 text, Jevons's 1877 second edition, Venn's 1888 third, the 1902 and 1913 translations of Laplace and Poincaré). Peirce's and Poincaré's quotations are dated by the essay or book they come from.
      • Mill and Darwin are marginal witnesses: their books are on the shelf for other reasons, and the table says so.
      • Verification proves a quotation exists, not that the stance is right. Irony and views stated in order to be refuted are marked where found (Mill's objector, Poincaré's "chance is only the measure of our ignorance"), and context is one click away.
