    About this prototype
    Evidence Ledger
    
    
  
    
  
    What it demonstrates
    
  
    Reading a literature as evidence on one proposition rather than as a pile of documents. Every quantitative claim in the relevant abstracts is extracted with its verbatim span, plotted against human benchmarks over time, and each paper becomes a likelihood ratio you can inspect and override. The posterior updates like a running bank balance as you tick papers read, and a sensitivity view shows which entries, and which modelling assumptions, carry the result.
    
  
    What is real
    
  
      
    
    • Papers: titles, authors, dates and abstracts from papers.json, 1,457 arXiv records (metadata CC0) fetched from the arXiv API on 15 October 2026. Every entry links to its arXiv page.
      • 
    
    • Quotes: every span shown in quotation marks is verbatim from the abstract. The build script (scripts/prototypes/evidence-ledger.mjs) checks each one by exact substring match after whitespace normalisation and drops any that fail; the live panel runs the same check in the browser. TeX escapes in arXiv abstracts (\%, $…$) are rendered as symbols.
      • 
    
    • Arithmetic: the Bayes update, dependence discount, running balance, sensitivity sweep and Squiggle export are computed in the page.
      • 
  
    
  
    What is AI-generated (precomputed)
    
  
    Claude read the 200 abstracts in the forecasting theme plus 35 keyword hits elsewhere, in October 2026, and wrote the extractions: metric, value, baseline, dataset, the claim's standing against human benchmarks, the proposed LR and its plausible band, the one-line rationales, the dependence groups and the set-aside reasons. Precomputed by Claude, October 2026 (AI-generated; spot-check before relying on it). Every such value is marked "AI-proposed" until you change it.
    
  
    Live generation
    
  
    Opened in Claude, the "Read another abstract" panel sends one abstract to Claude through the artifact's sample capability and gets back claims, a proposed LR and quoted spans. Nothing is plotted unless its span matches the abstract and its figure appears in the span. Elsewhere (for example on the static wiki) the panel shows three precomputed examples instead.
    
  
    Method and its limits
    
  
    posterior odds = prior odds × Π LRᵢ^wᵢ
    wᵢ = 1 for the strongest entry in a group, 1 − d for the others
    
  
      
    
    • Abstracts only. Abstracts overclaim and omit figures; the AIA parity claim, for instance, gives no number. LRs read off abstracts are crude, so the slider moves in coarse steps (×0.2 to ×5) and each LR carries a band.
      • 
    
    • Standing is a judgement. Placing a claim "below crowd" or "≈ superforecasters" compresses different metrics and benchmarks onto one ordinal scale. Hover any mark for the underlying figure.
      • 
    
    • Dependence is a heuristic. Papers sharing a benchmark, comparison design or team are grouped; the knob discounts all but the strongest entry in each group. It swings the answer a lot, which is the point.
      • 
    
    • Recency matters. The proposition is about October 2026. A 2023 shortfall is nearly as likely in a world where today's systems reach parity, so older entries get LRs near 1.
      • 
    
    • Corpus. arXiv only, as fetched; no journals, blogs or leaderboards (ForecastBench's live leaderboard is not in the data).
      • 
    
    • Nothing is mocked except the stand-in shown when live generation is unavailable, which is a labelled precomputed example.
      •
