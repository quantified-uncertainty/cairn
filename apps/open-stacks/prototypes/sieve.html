      About

      Sieve is a light prototype for Open Stacks. It revives Scatter/Gather: instead of guessing search words, you look at nine piles, keep the promising ones, and the machine re-sorts what you kept into nine finer piles, as many times as you like.

      Cutting, D. R., Karger, D. R., Pedersen, J. O. & Tukey, J. W. (1992). Scatter/Gather: a cluster-based approach to browsing large document collections. Proc. SIGIR ’92, 318–329. doi:10.1145/133160.133214

      Pirolli, Schank, Hearst & Diehl (CHI ’96, doi:10.1145/238386.238489) found it worse than keyword search for finding a known item, but better at teaching the shape of a collection and the words to search it with. The weak link was the cluster labels, which were lists of words. Here Claude writes the labels.

      How it works

      • Features. Title and subtitle words, subject headings (as words and as whole phrases) and the LC class letters (for example Q and QA), weighted by TF-IDF. The IDF is recomputed on every gathered set, so words shared by the whole set stop counting.
      • Clustering. Spherical k-means, k = 9, in a Web Worker in your browser. Seeds come from k-means++ driven by a random number generator keyed to a hash of the gathered set, with 4–10 restarts. The same gather therefore always gives the same piles.
      • Layout. The nine trays are placed on the 3 × 3 grid so that neighbouring trays have the most similar centroids.
      • Heaps. Each chit is one work, coloured by its ebook access. Heaps settle by a sand-pile rule (angle of repose), so a pile’s height shows its size. Chits get bigger as the sieve gets finer.
      • Representatives. Four works from the most central 30% of each pile, ranked by Open Library reading-log count, with duplicate titles removed.
      • Distinctive terms. The terms most over-represented in a pile relative to the whole gathered set.

      Real data

      • 7,127 works from the Open Library Search API (CC0), fetched 15 October 2026 through 50 subject queries around decisions, uncertainty, statistics, AI, economics, philosophy and science. It is a slice, not a whole library.
      • Access status, call numbers, years and reading-log counts are Open Library’s own. LC class names come from the Library of Congress Classification Outline.

      Generated by Claude

      • Precomputed: names and one-line descriptions for the first nine piles and for the nine piles you get by gathering any one of them (90 piles). Written by Claude in October 2026 from each pile’s terms and typical titles. They are AI-generated, so spot-check them.
      • Live: when this page is opened in Claude, every new scatter sends one batched request for nine names and lines. Claude sees only the terms, LC classes and typical titles of each pile.
      • Fallback: without Claude, deeper piles are named from their two most distinctive terms, shown in capitals.

      Not built here

      • No search box, no steering by text, and no papers or Gutenberg records mixed in. The fuller design is in the gallery.
