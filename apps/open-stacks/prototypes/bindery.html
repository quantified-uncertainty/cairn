    About this prototype

    The Bindery

    A deep mockup of a tool that turns a one-line brief into a bound volume: a sequence of real chapters, papers and textbook sections, with an editor’s note before each part, sized to the hours you have. Its second view, Route, drills from a recent paper down through the textbooks and classics it rests on, then solves for the cheapest path back up.

    What it demonstrates

    • Transclusion, not generation. Every piece is a record: a chapter of a public-domain text (readable here), an arXiv paper, an OpenStax section, a free textbook named in the R01 dossier, or an Open Library work with a chapter chosen from a fetched table of contents.
    • Hours as a budget. The slider runs a deterministic allocator in the page: each piece can be bound as a gist, skimmed, read or studied, and the allocator buys depth by value per minute. The spine thickens and thins with the pages bound.
    • Honest access routes. Access keys (open only, plus library loans, plus purchases) swap unreachable pieces for free substitutes and say what each swap loses. “Borrowable” is checked against the language of the actual lendable scan.
    • A resolver gate. The bench log shows every check: ids resolved, page ranges read from editions, quotations verified verbatim, and candidates rejected because they are not in the corpus.

    Real data

    • Open Library catalog (7,127 works, CC0), arXiv metadata (1,457 papers, CC0), OpenStax book list and section archive, Project Gutenberg catalog and nine full texts, all fetched 15 October 2026.
    • Tables of contents and page ranges from Open Library editions; Internet Archive scan languages; arXiv page counts; OpenStax section word counts; three further Gutenberg excerpts (Pascal, Clifford, FitzRoy). Fetched and checked by scripts/prototypes/bindery.mjs.
    • Free textbook URLs (FPP3, Jaynes, MacKay, Hardt & Recht, Grinstead & Snell) from research/R01-open-books.md, re-checked on 15 October 2026.

    AI-generated (precomputed)

    The three volumes’ titles, editor’s notes, one-line reasons, substitution notes, route concepts and “what this layer adds” lines were written by Claude in October 2026 and are labelled in the page. They are grounded in titles, tables of contents and abstracts; spot-check them before relying on them. Quotations inside notes are verified verbatim by the build script.

    Live, when opened in Claude

    “Bind with Claude as editor” asks Claude (through the artifact’s sample capability) to search the catalog, papers, chapters and textbooks with page tools and return a volume; any id that does not resolve is rejected and listed. Without Claude, “Bind from the catalogue” builds an unedited volume by keyword match, with no notes.

    Mocked or simplified

    • Reading times are estimates from word or page counts; arXiv papers without a stated length are assumed to be 14 pages.
    • No prices: “buy” means buy or use a physical library; nothing is checked against a bookshop.
    • The drawn-path gesture over a map of knowledge, schedule export and lecture segments from the panel spec are not built.
    • Route prerequisite links are editorial judgements (“conceptual ancestor in this corpus”), not citations.
