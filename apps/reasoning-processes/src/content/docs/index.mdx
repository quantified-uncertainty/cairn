Notes on measuring reasoning processes such as peer review, prediction markets, audits and LLM judges: what they deliver, what they cost, and what it costs to corrupt them. This is an early research exploration, with drafts and conjectures rather than settled results.

Start with [Chapter 1](/start-here/introduction/), or go straight to [The Process Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/).

## The map

Each process gets two numbers: what it costs to run, per bit of validated information, and what an adversary must pay to bend its output. Plotting the catalogue’s 17 processes on those two axes leaves one corner empty: nothing yet is both cheap to run and hard to corrupt.

<HomeMap>

Shapes are the catalogue’s three families: <span class="fam"><svg class="key" viewBox="0 0 10 10" aria-hidden="true"><circle cx="5" cy="5" r="4.5"/></svg>grounded in resolution or track records,</span> <span class="fam"><svg class="key" viewBox="0 0 10 10" aria-hidden="true"><rect x="1" y="1" width="8" height="8"/></svg>institutional judgment,</span> <span class="fam"><svg class="key" viewBox="0 0 10 10" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M5 .5L10 9.5H0Z"/></svg>AI-native.</span> Positions are our own rough judgments from [The Process Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/), so the empty corner is a conjecture to test, not a finding.

The arrows show the two directions in which [hardening](/concepts/hardening-techniques/) can move a cheap process. Hardening bought with verification costs work on every run, so it raises both costs and stays on the diagonal. [Deterrence](/concepts/hardening-deterrence/) (bonds, clawbacks, occasional audits) raises the cost to corrupt while adding little to the cost to run, which is why it looks like the most promising route into the corner. Neither move has been measured.

## How to read these notes

- **Formalisms are graded** [exact], [standard shape] or [heuristic], by how far each can be trusted. See [The Core Model](/concepts/core-model/).
- **Failures are published.** [LLM Epistemics in Production](/case-studies/llm-epistemics-in-production/) reports false positives, overconfidence and a miscounted competition alongside the wins.
