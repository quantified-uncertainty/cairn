## The idea in one paragraph

**Robust reasoning processes (RRP)** is our working name for procedures that turn effort and evidence into trustworthy conclusions — *and keep doing so when interested parties try to corrupt them*. The objects are processes, not people or models: peer review, prediction markets, audits, LLM judge pipelines, debate protocols. The questions are about measurement: what does a process deliver, what does it cost, and what does it cost to corrupt? Many processes society relies on were never benchmarked. AI makes them easier to study — an LLM-based process can be re-run, perturbed, and attacked on purpose — and more urgent, because plausible reasoning is now cheap to produce and still costly to verify. These pages are exploratory and brainstorm-heavy: a synthesis of mechanism design, scalable oversight, forecasting science, and security economics, plus conjectures, not a settled discipline.

## The map

Every process gets two headline numbers: the cost of extracting validated information from it, and the cost an adversary pays to bend its output. Plotting the [Process Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/) on that plane gives a rough map. The coordinates are our qualitative judgments, so the quadrant it shows as empty is a conjecture to test, not a finding.

## The three layers

- **The Judge** — the consumer of reasoning, who cannot check the work object-level. What they need: a utility function over information, a way to price information against it, and the process-conditional weights that say how much each source should move them.
- **The Processes** — the procedures themselves: their anatomy, what their verdicts ground out in, their measured properties, and the attack-and-hardening cycle that makes them robust.
- **The Environment** — the game the processes live inside: producers, resolvers, accreditors, adversaries; the identity and track-record infrastructure every incentive scheme silently assumes; and the market, legal, and cultural constraints.

## Norms these notes try to follow

1. **Formalisms are graded.** Every equation carries a grade — **[exact]** (correct by construction), **[standard shape]** (precedent in an adjacent rigorous field), or **[heuristic]** (a deliberately simple decomposition, not a law). See [The Core Model](/concepts/core-model/).
2. **Failures are published.** The deployment write-ups in [LLM Epistemics in Production](/case-studies/llm-epistemics-in-production/) report false-positive rates, overconfidence, and a miscounted competition alongside the wins.
3. **Claims are provisional.** Everything here is a working draft, stated so it can be argued with. The measurements these notes propose are mostly still to come: the catalogue's ratings are qualitative judgments, and no incentive audit has been run.
