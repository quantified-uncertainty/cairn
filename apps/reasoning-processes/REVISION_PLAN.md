# Robust Reasoning Processes — Revision Plan

*September 2026. Written after a full read of all 24 pages (~35k words).
Phase 1 shipped in the PR that added this file; the rest is proposed.*

## Decision

RRP is the stronger of the two wikis: dense, well cited, honest about
grades. Its problems are **accretion** and **incompleteness**, not
quality. Prose has picked up caveats with each review pass, cross-references
drifted as chapters were renumbered, and the Environment layer (Part IV),
which three chapters call the field's most leveraged missing piece, was
entirely unwritten. The plan: finish the book's missing layer, fix
drift structurally, then cut the prose back.

**What would flip the order:** if the book is about to be shared widely,
do the readability pass (Phase 2) before new chapters (Phase 3). First
impressions are made by Chapters 1–7, not Part IV.

## Diagnosis (what the read found)

1. **Cross-reference drift after renumbering.**
   - The glossary defined *deception affordance* backwards: "near one …
     transmits persuasion". The owning chapter says near 1 means
     information.
   - The glossary and The Funding Effect still used $\tau$, which the
     Untrustworthy Sources rewrite retired. The Funding Effect still
     called Ch. 6 an interlude.
   - The Process Catalogue cited The Funding Effect for an example that
     page doesn't contain.
   - The Hardening overview promised a capstone chapter the ToC doesn't
     have.
   - The hand-maintained `llms.txt` numbered Chapters 7–10 one lower
     than the ToC, still called Ch. 6 an interlude, and linked to no
     full-text export.
2. **Rendering bugs on the live site.** The Deterrence chapter's worked
   bound rendered as red KaTeX parse errors (`\$` inside math), and one
   anchor link was dead.
3. **An understated empirical citation.** Oversight Protocols said
   forecasters captured "roughly 72% of the benefit-cost value". Accurate,
   but it hid that this cost *more* than the evaluator (120%), and that
   the cheap crowd had negative value.
4. **Part IV entirely planned.** Deterrence, incentive-compatibility,
   independence, and the Catalogue all end at "needs identity
   infrastructure".
5. **Prose density (measured).** Mean 26.9 words per sentence and 66 words
   per paragraph, vs. 22.1 and 47 in the sibling wiki. Six chapters exceed
   31 words per sentence. The Process Catalogue has a 287-word paragraph
   and Constructing Utility Functions a 200-word one. Most of the excess
   is inline caveats: parentheticals and dashes run at 3.6 per 100 words,
   and 6.1 in Oversight Protocols.
6. **Duplicated passages.** The min-cut composition argument appears in
   both the Process Catalogue and the Hardening overview. The
   tail-calibration paragraph appears in both the Hardening overview and
   the Calibration chapter.
7. **Stale maintainer doc.** CLAUDE.md described an autogenerated sidebar
   that no longer exists.

## Phase 1 — shipped in this PR

- All drift items in (1) and the rendering bugs in (2) fixed. The
  amplification citation restated with its cost side (3).
- **New Ch. 19, Identity and Track-Record Infrastructure.** It covers:
  - binding vs. counting;
  - the enforceable-penalty decomposition $B_{\text{eff}} \approx B_{\text{escrow}} + R$,
    priced with the Deterrence chapter's own $S^\*$ formula ("a track
    record is a bond that cannot be withdrawn");
  - what makes a track record expensive to fake;
  - what changes for copyable AI processes (continuity cheap, counting

  - a constructions table.

- `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` generated from the sidebar at build time
  (the drift in (1) can't recur). `scripts/check-built-links.py` checks
  links, anchors, and KaTeX errors across both wikis. CLAUDE.md
  corrected.

## Phase 2 — readability pass

Targets (measured with the same script): ≤ 24 words per sentence, no
paragraph over 150 words, asides ≤ 2.5 per 100 words. Method, per page:

- Lead each section with the claim in one plain sentence.
- Move caveats into a short **Limits** list after the claim instead of
  threading them through it. Don't delete them: most are correct and were
  added in response to review.
- Merge the two duplicated passages in (6) into their owning chapter;
  replace the copies with pointers.
- Order: Process Catalogue, Reliability Ladder, Constructing Utility
  Functions, Oversight Protocols, Hardening overview, Funding Effect.

**Obstacle:** caveats are load-bearing for the book's credibility, and a
careless pass would reintroduce the overclaims review removed. Each page
needs a diff reviewed by the author, not a bulk rewrite.

## Phase 3 — finish the book

- **Ch. 18 Certification and Gyms**, with the promised capstone: one
  hardened stack (consistency floor → multi-model panel → randomized
  expert escalation) on a real task, with its corruption cost
  *measured* by a paid red team. This is the Catalogue's "What v1 needs"
  item 4, and the first incentive audit.
- **Ch. 20 The Market for Truth** — who pays for verification; draws on
  the certification economics in EE's Patterns §3.
- **Ch. 21 Law, Liability, and Private Deliberation.**
- **Ch. 22 Culture, Consent, and Adoption** — write once, shared with EE's
  Epistemic Culture chapter rather than duplicating it.
- **Obstacle:** Ch. 18 needs money for the red team; the others need
  research sweeps comparable to the ones behind EE's catalogue.

## Phase 4 — measurement: Process Catalogue v1

The book's own stated central project. Start where the data is cheapest:

1. **Label-swap neutrality** for 3–5 LLM judges: a no-ground-truth
   measurement of $b_\pi(D)$ that can run this month.
2. **Funding-effect likelihood-ratio discount** from the existing
   meta-science corpus (the case study's "Toward a measured version").
3. **Correlated error** across model families, to put a number on the
   Hardening overview's "largest undefended threat".

Each result replaces one L/M/H cell with an interval.

## Phase 5 — structural hygiene

- Move chapters into part-based directories (`judge/`, `processes/`,
  `hardening/`, `environment/`, `applications/`) with Astro `redirects`
  for every old URL. The directories no longer match the Parts
  (Chapter 4 lives in `proposals/`, Chapter 25 in `case-studies/`).
  Low reader value; do it only alongside a larger edit.
- Run `scripts/check-built-links.py` in CI after the builds.
- Drop the ignored `sidebar.order` frontmatter.

## Not in scope

- New formalisms in the Core Model. Its grades are the agenda, and
  estimation methods for the [exact] parts matter more than new equations.
