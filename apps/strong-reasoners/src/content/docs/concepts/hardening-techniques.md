*The constructive half of the field: given the [Core Model's](/concepts/core-model/) quantities, how do you engineer good values of them? One objective — eliminate confident error — and two levers that achieve it: minimize the output's sensitivity to anything the agent or adversary controls, and maximize the penalty an adversary can be made to pay. The empty quadrant of the [Process Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/) is reachable, but only via the second lever, and machine reasoners permit applying both at an intensity no human consultant would tolerate.*

## The objective: eliminate confident error

The only epistemic state that can hurt a judge *unboundedly* is being confidently wrong. A process that is calibrated — whose stated confidence $c$ means it is wrong a $(1-c)$ fraction of the time — converts an open-ended tail risk into a bounded, priceable one: the judge sizes its commitment to $c$, and the expected damage is insurable. Overconfidence is the sole channel for unbounded surprise, and a deceiver is, by construction, *confidently wrong* — deception is a calibration failure in the tail. So the safety-relevant quantity is not overall accuracy or Brier score but **tail calibration**: $P(\text{false} \mid \text{the process asserts} \ge c)$ for $c$ near 1. **[heuristic]**

This reframes the field's hardest measurement target into a more winnable one. "Make the process accurate" may be impossible; "make the process never confidently wrong" is achievable, because a process can always widen its intervals or abstain. The relevant literature is recent and specific: a forecast can be perfectly calibrated on average yet badly miscalibrated in its tail, which is exactly the dangerous regime ([Allen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03167)), and tail reliability can be enforced at training time with weighted scoring rules ([Wessel et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13687)). Large models already "mostly know what they know" on tractable formats ([Kadavath et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)), and the gap to close is principled abstention with a cost — risk-coverage selective prediction ([Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)), refusal-tuning ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09677)), distribution-free thresholds via [conformal risk control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814) — paired with a sharpness floor so calibration is not bought by retreating to base rates (the reliability–resolution decomposition of [Murphy 1973](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0450(1973)012%3C0595:ANVPOT%3E2.0.CO;2); maximize sharpness *subject to* calibration, [Gneiting et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x)). The bridge to the rest of this book is [U-calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00168) ([Kleinberg et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00168)): calibration equals worst-case regret over *all* bounded proper scoring rules, so a calibrated process is one no downstream decision-maker — or bettor — can systematically exploit.

## Two ways to raise corruption cost

The [corruption cost curve](/concepts/core-model/#4-corruption-standard-shape) can be raised in exactly two ways, and the distinction is the chapter's organizing claim:

- **Verification (on-equilibrium).** Build redundant independent checks into the output. Each check an adversary must defeat multiplies the corruption cost — *but each also costs real information-extraction work every time the process runs.* Verification raises both of the field's axes together: you move up-and-right along the efficient frontier and never reach the cheap-and-incorruptible corner.
- **Deterrence (off-equilibrium).** Bonds, clawbacks, tamper-evidence, randomized escalation. These cost almost nothing on the honest path — you rarely escalate; a bond is posted collateral — yet impose a large cost on an adversary. Deterrence raises corruption cost *without* raising information cost. It is the only move that goes left-and-up, into the [Catalogue's](/concepts/process-catalogue/) empty quadrant.

Everything that already lives in that quadrant in the wider economy lives there by deterrence, not verification: financial audit (rarely audited, but the penalty is ruin — the classical economics of [deterrence as detection × penalty](https://doi.org/10.1086/259394), Becker 1968), certificate transparency, deposit-and-slash staking, criminal law. The corollary reframes the Catalogue's headline finding: **the empty quadrant is a deterrence-engineering problem, and identity/track-record infrastructure is the field's most leveraged missing institution because it sets the maximum enforceable penalty.**

The two subsections below take the levers in turn; a third covers the machine affordances that let us push both far past the human baseline.

## Lever A — minimize sensitivity to anything the agent controls

A robust process's output should be a function of the truth-relevant variables and (near-)invariant to the nuisance variables an adversary or the agent can set: prompt phrasing, format, order, persona, the choice of which study to run, the framing of the question. Formally, the **corruption gain** of a process is the Lipschitz constant of its output with respect to adversary-controllable inputs, $L = \lVert \partial(\text{output}) / \partial(\text{adversary levers}) \rVert$, and the achievable distortion is bounded by $\Delta \le L \cdot (\text{reachable perturbation budget})$. Minimizing $L$ is the design objective. **[standard shape]**

This is not new mathematics; it is **robust statistics**, imported. The influence function *is* the per-input sensitivity, and the **breakdown point** — the fraction of inputs an adversary must corrupt to move the output arbitrarily far — is an off-the-shelf, single-number corruption-robustness metric ([Huber 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732); the influence-function programme of Hampel, Ronchetti, Rousseeuw & Stahel 1986; the breakdown point of Donoho & Huber 1983; ~50%-breakdown estimators, [Rousseeuw 1984](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1984.10477105)). The mean has breakdown 0; the median, 50%. The aggregation literature makes the consequence sharp: **no linear aggregation rule tolerates even a single adversarial input** ([Blanchard et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02757), introducing Krum), which is the rigorous form of "averaging LLM judges is maximally corruptible." Bounded-breakdown replacements — coordinate-wise median and trimmed mean with optimal rates ([Yin et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01498)), high-dimensional robust mean estimation ([Diakonikolas et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06443)) — are the drop-in primitives. And differential privacy is the same property viewed from the other side: it bounds the output's sensitivity to any one contributor by $\varepsilon$, so an adversary controlling $k$ inputs moves the output at most $\sim k\varepsilon$ ([Dwork et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/11681878_14); composition theorems in [Dwork & Roth 2014](https://doi.org/10.1561/0400000042)); [Dwork & Lei (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1536414.1536466) prove influence-function robustness and DP sensitivity are two views of one low-distortion property.

Concrete constructions on this lever:

- **Breakdown-point ratings.** Rate every aggregating process by the fraction of corrupted inputs it survives, and prefer median/trimmed/robust-mean pooling to averaging. A direct, quantitative column for the [Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/).
- **Influence audits and DP ceilings.** Numerically estimate each source's influence, cap any single source's pull (bounded-influence pooling), or add calibrated noise so no contributor can move the conclusion past a threshold.
- **Randomize-everything harness.** Randomize phrasing, format, order, persona, and decoding seed per query and report only the invariant; the *variance* across randomization is itself the corruption-exposure metric. This is what [opinion fuzzing](/concepts/untrustworthy-sources/#closing-the-residual-channels) does, named as sensitivity reduction.
- **Identity-masking gap.** Re-run with the producer's affiliation masked versus revealed; any gap is live corruption surface — the empirical handle on the [funding effect](/case-studies/the-funding-effect/).

The irreducible limit is correlated error: aggregation, peer prediction, and multi-model panels all assume some independence, and shared training pipelines can make a whole population confidently wrong together — a sensitivity that no single-source bound captures.

## Lever B — maximize the enforceable penalty

Where verification cannot be afforded, deterrence substitutes. The canonical instrument is randomized escalation with a bond. Wrap a cheap process (corruption cost $c$) in an audit that, with probability $p$, escalates to an expensive robust process, and require participants to post an enforceable bond $B$ forfeited on detection. Modeling this as a classic inspection game gives a frontier: **[standard shape]**

$$\text{expected info cost per item} = c_{\text{info}} + p \cdot C_{\text{info}}(\text{expensive}); \qquad \text{safe-stake ceiling} \;\; S^\* = \frac{c + pB}{1 - p}.$$

The honest path pays the first quantity; an adversary faces the second. The decisive term is $B$: with $B = 0$ (today's bare LLM-judge world) $S^\* = c/(1-p)$, so robustness requires $p \to 1$ — full verification — which is exactly the trap cheap processes are in. With $B$ large, $S^\*$ grows without bound for *any* $p > 0$. **Robustness is bounded by the maximum enforceable penalty, not by verification spend.** This is why bets, bonds, and identity-bound reputation are hardening moves, not mere accounting: forcing a model to quote odds and stake on them (e.g. "90%, willing to lay 10:1 up to a capped stake") simultaneously elicits honest probabilities, bounds the [corruption surplus](/concepts/core-model/#4-corruption-standard-shape), and sets $B$.

Deterrence is cheapest when detection can be driven toward certainty *after the fact*, decoupling robustness from audit cost (the Core Model's "detection can substitute for prevention"):

- **Merkle-logged reasoning traces.** Hash-commit every reasoning step to an append-only log so any post-hoc audit detects tampering with probability ~1 — certificate transparency for cognition ([RFC 6962](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962); Merkle history trees, [Crosby & Wallach 2009](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/sec09/tech/full_papers/crosby.pdf)).
- **A PCP-analogue for estimates.** All cheap robustness runs on verification being cheaper than production — the asymmetry behind SAT, proof-carrying code ([Necula 1997](https://doi.org/10.1145/263699.263712)), succinct verifiable computation ([Pinocchio, Parno et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2013.47)), and the PCP theorem, under which a proof can be checked by reading $O(1)$ random bits ([Arora & Safra 1998](https://doi.org/10.1145/273865.273901); [Arora et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1145/278298.278306); gap-amplification re-proof, [Dinur 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1236457.1236459)). For *proofs* the field has Lean; for *estimates* there is no analogue — you cannot spot-check a probability. The open construction: require a structured decomposition (a Fermi tree, a Squiggle model, a causal graph) as a checkable certificate, and spot-check a fraction $f$ of its nodes, giving detection $\ge 1 - (1-f)^{g(\Delta)}$ for a distortion forced across $g(\Delta)$ nodes. This is the dual of the counterexample-shrinking idea in [Consistency Evaluations](/proposals/consistency-evals/) (delta-debugging, [Zeller & Hildebrandt 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2002.988498)).
- **Commit-reveal forecasting.** Sealed hashed forecasts revealed simultaneously ([Naor 1991](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00196774); [Blum 1981](https://doi.org/10.1145/1008908.1008911)) remove the copy channel, so herding cannot collapse the effective independent-sample count and the cheap "move the visible median to anchor others" attack disappears — improving both axes at once.
- **n-version reasoning and provenance bonds.** Require agreement across independently built reasoning stacks ([Avizienis 1985](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.1985.231893)) — discounted by *measured* shared-training correlation, never assumed independent — and make every cited source post a stake slashed if it is later shown corrupt, pricing the [funding-effect](/case-studies/the-funding-effect/) discount into the mechanism.

A standing caveat from the Core Model: a rational judge is not a defense. Controlling the *information structure* can manipulate even a fully Bayesian receiver, so the corruption channel is closed by controlling who designs the process and verifying what was run — never by judge intelligence alone.

## The enabling asymmetry: machine-oversight affordances

The [Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/) is a list of processes evolved for *humans* — who have rights, privacy, fatigue, and exist as single un-forkable instances. None of those constraints bind for an AI reasoner, and the slack is the field's largest untapped resource. We can subject a machine reasoner to oversight no consultant would tolerate, and **each affordance is either a Lever-A move (fewer controllable degrees of freedom) or a Lever-B move (a larger enforceable penalty):**

| Affordance | Lever | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| Reset / re-run from scratch | A | mind-wiped re-derivation: stable conclusions reproduce, motivated ones drift |
| Fork mid-reasoning | A | checkpoint-fork cross-examination down $k$ non-communicating branches |
| Decorrelated resampling | A | confession only counts if invariant across framings (sequential-test thresholding) |
| Sandboxed information diet | A | reject any conclusion resting on un-logged retrievals |
| Narrow, fixed action space | A | lower the Lipschitz $L$ by removing levers |
| Full logging / no privacy | B | tamper-evident reasoning trace, free |
| Forced bets / bonds | B | set $B$; exponentially remove overconfident agents |
| Read internal state | A/B | activation-level honesty checks |

**Intense interrogation** is the headline construction, and the machine affordances are what make its false-positive problem tractable. A coerced confession is highly *sensitive* to the interrogator's framing (you can push any model into "confessing"); a real defect is invariant across resets and decorrelated framings — so a confession should be defined as a low-sensitivity invariant, not a single admission. Threshold on the fraction of $m$ decorrelated probes that elicit it: both error rates drop toward zero whenever the bad-model confession rate exceeds the fine-but-pushed rate, and the entire research question reduces to designing probes with that separating property. The behavioral primitives exist: black-box lie detection by asking unrelated follow-ups ([Pacchiardi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15840)).

The "read their minds" affordance is the highest-leverage and the **least reliable**, and must be graded honestly. Truth appears to be linearly represented in activations ([Marks & Tegmark 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06824)) and linear probes catch strategic deception at high AUROC ([Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407)) — but chain-of-thought is often *unfaithful* to the real computation ([Turpin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388); [Lanham et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702); reasoning models verbalize decisive hints under 20% of the time, [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410); unfaithful CoT occurs in the wild, [Arcuschin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08679)); deception can survive safety training ([Sleeper Agents, Hubinger et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566)) and capability can be hidden on purpose ([Sandbagging, van der Weij et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358)). Worst for any scheme that *trains* on an honesty signal: optimizing against an internals-based lie detector can induce genuine honesty *or* detector-evading deception depending on setup ([Cundy et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13787)). Hence **[speculative]**: use activation honesty as corroboration, never as the load-bearing check.

## Forced precision: making lies expensive to keep coherent

A complementary construction turns a bold claim into a high-resolution commitment. Demand that a claim be cashed into a precise, preregistered operationalization *and* a comprehensive probabilistic model connecting it to checkable facts. By the Quine–Duhem structure, a false claim can always be saved by adjusting auxiliary assumptions — but maintaining it coherent at resolution $R$ costs $k_R$ improbable auxiliary commitments, and the defended position's joint prior decays like $p^{k_R}$. The mechanism therefore forces a confident liar into one of two states: lower the confidence (back to the chapter's objective) or expose a checkable contradiction. It "breaks down for many claims" precisely where there are too few checkable connections — the genuinely unresolvable residue that [judge-grounded protocols](/concepts/oversight-protocols/) exist for. **[heuristic]**

## What this chapter needs

The grades are the agenda. The **[standard shape]** imports (breakdown point, DP, the inspection-game frontier) need their assumptions stress-tested for information attacks rather than the cryptographic or statistical settings they were proved in. The **[speculative]** affordances (activation honesty, interrogation) need protocols with demonstrated separating power and decorrelated probes. And the whole chapter inherits the field's central uncertainty: every penalty bottoms out in some resolution layer, and deterrence works only as far as detection is eventually real.

## Open questions

- Does composition actually multiply corruption costs, or merely add the weakest link? The inspection-game frontier predicts a value for randomized escalation; the [Catalogue](/concepts/process-catalogue/) flags this as the central unmeasured composition question.
- Is there a usable PCP-analogue for quantitative estimates — an encoding under which any large output distortion forces detectable local inconsistency in a constant number of spot-checkable nodes?
- Can an interrogation protocol be built whose confession rate genuinely separates corrupted from honest-but-pushed reasoners, with probes decorrelated enough that artifacts do not correlate?
- How much enforceable penalty $B$ is reachable without identity infrastructure, and how much does that infrastructure raise $S^\*$ across the Catalogue's rows?
- Which oversight affordances degrade capability when imposed (narrow action spaces, capability throttling), and what is the robustness-for-capability exchange rate?
