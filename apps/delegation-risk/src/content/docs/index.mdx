This site treats the risk of handing work to people, software and, above all, AI agents as a quantity: something to estimate before acting, budget across a system and design to keep small.

<p class="status"><strong>Epistemic status:</strong> early-stage, exploratory work, written quickly with LLM assistance. The core ideas seem promising, but the framework is largely untested on real AI systems and most numbers on the site are illustrative. ## The idea in brief

**Risk is a number you can budget.** For each way a delegation can go wrong, multiply its probability by the damage it would do, and add up the results:

<p class="formula">Delegation risk = <span>Σ P(harm mode) × Damage</span></p>

A research assistant that might leak proprietary data (P = 0.001 a month, damage \$50,000) contributes \$50 a month. Harm arrives through three channels: [accident, defection and hijack](/delegation-risk/risk-decomposition/).

**Stacked checks fail together.** Three checks that each miss 10% of harmful actions promise a 0.1% miss rate if they fail independently. If they share a common cause with weight ρ (the same model, data or context), the [beta‑factor correction](/delegation-risk/risk-propagation/) gives a miss rate of (1−ρ)·0.001 + ρ·0.1. That is about 11 times the promised rate at ρ&nbsp;=&nbsp;0.1, 21 times at 0.2 and 50 times at 0.5. This multiplier is the [entanglement tax](/entanglements/).

Counted in independent checks, three are worth about 2.0 at ρ&nbsp;=&nbsp;0.1, 1.7 at 0.2 and 1.3 at 0.5. No study has measured ρ for checks on harmful actions. Error correlations on benchmarks suggest about 0.3–0.6 for LLMs from different providers and 0.5–0.7 for models from one provider. Against an attacker who searches for inputs that defeat every layer at once, assume ρ is near&nbsp;1: three checks are then worth one.

<EntanglementFigure />

**Do the accounting at runtime.** Before a consequential action, the agent’s harness runs a [risk preflight](/delegation-risk/runtime-accounting/) that estimates expected damage. It enforces an [exposure envelope](/delegation-risk/runtime-accounting/#2-the-exposure-envelope) of hard limits that hold whatever the agent intends, and keeps an [exposure ledger](/delegation-risk/runtime-accounting/#3-the-exposure-ledger) reconciled against real incidents.
