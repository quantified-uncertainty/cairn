## The problem

Delegating a task to an employee, a script or an AI agent means accepting some downside in exchange for capability you lack. This site treats that downside as a quantity. For each way the delegation can go wrong, multiply the probability by the damage, and sum: [Delegation Risk = Σ P(harm mode) × Damage](/getting-started/core-concepts/#the-formula). With illustrative monthly figures, a research assistant that might leak proprietary data (P = 0.001, damage \$50,000) contributes \$50; a deployer that might ship malicious code (P = 0.0001, damage \$1,000,000) contributes \$100.

The number lets you compare delegates and safeguards without committing in advance to a remedy. It is only as good as the estimates behind it, and producing those is the hard part.

The site's main proposal is to move this accounting into the harness around an agent. Before a consequential action, the scaffold runs a [risk preflight](/delegation-risk/runtime-accounting/) that estimates the action's expected damage. It enforces an [exposure envelope](/delegation-risk/runtime-accounting/#2-the-exposure-envelope), a set of hard limits that hold regardless of the agent's intent. And it keeps an [exposure ledger](/delegation-risk/runtime-accounting/#3-the-exposure-ledger) that is reconciled against real incidents.

## The distinctive number

Stacked safeguards are rarely independent. Put three checks that each miss 10% of harmful actions in front of a risky action and the independence arithmetic promises a 0.1% miss rate. If the checks share a common cause with weight ρ (the same model family, training data or context), the [beta-factor correction](/delegation-risk/risk-propagation/) gives a miss rate of (1−ρ)·0.001 + ρ·0.1. That is 1 + 99ρ times the naive figure: about 11× at ρ = 0.1, 21× at ρ = 0.2 and 50× at ρ = 0.5. The multiplier is the [entanglement tax](/entanglements/).

Two caveats:

- **ρ is measured only on benchmarks.** Recomputed from Kim et al.'s released MMLU data ([arXiv:2506.07962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)), strong LLMs from different providers have ρ of about 0.3–0.6 on their misses and models from one provider about 0.5–0.7, so 50× is a plausible central value ([Measuring Entanglement](/entanglements/detection/correlation-calculator/#what-the-evidence-supports)). Nothing comparable has been measured for harm detection.
- **It is benign-failure math.** Against an attacker who searches for inputs that defeat every layer at once, assume ρ near 1 unless a control evaluation shows otherwise.

## Why AI systems

The framework applies to any delegation, but AI agents are the focus:

- **Capability is growing quickly, and verification is not obviously keeping pace.** The length of software tasks (in human working time) that frontier models complete with 50% reliability doubled roughly every seven months from 2019 to early 2025 ([Kwa et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499)).
- **Agents delegate to other agents** through tools, subagents and third-party servers, forming networks that nobody fully maps.
- **There is economic pressure toward autonomy** ([Gwern 2016](https://gwern.net/tool-ai)).

Harm arrives through three channels: [accident, defection and hijack](/delegation-risk/risk-decomposition/). Hijack is a third party steering a delegate that is not itself scheming, often through instructions planted in a document, email, web page, tool description or tool output. OWASP ranks prompt injection first among LLM application risks ([LLM01:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)). The aim is containment, not alignment: to bound the damage any single component can do, and to stop safety architectures from promising more than they deliver.

The work is complementary to [AI Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942) (Greenblatt et al. 2023). AI Control designs protocols meant to stay safe even if the model is scheming, and the adversarial methods for testing them. Delegation engineering is the accounting and architecture layer those protocols would be budgeted in, and it also covers the accident and hijack channels. [Related Approaches](/reference/related-approaches/) gives the full positioning.
