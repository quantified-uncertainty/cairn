## The field in one paragraph

**Evaluation engineering**, as this wiki uses the term, is the design of *systems* that produce large numbers of estimates and evaluations at known cost: grant reviews, charity assessments, research triage, policy appraisals. The unit of analysis is not one evaluation ("how good was this grant?") but the machinery that produces them by the thousand: who staffs it, what data it runs on, how it is audited, how updates propagate, and who pays for it. The bet is that the bottleneck has moved from evaluating one thing well to evaluating ten thousand things well enough, cheaply enough, that the results get used. The wiki's own evidence adds a constraint: in a coding of about 160 standing systems, those paid by the parties they rate had about three times the odds of a documented failure ([catalogue](/concepts/evaluation-systems-in-the-wild/#does-who-pays-predict-failure)). So the design variables are institutional as well as technical ([Evaluation as a System](/concepts/the-systems-view/)).

The core pattern is old and has precedents outside this project: a cheap layer predicts an expensive, trusted evaluator, and a hidden random subset is audited. Its relatives include automated essay scoring, DARPA's replication-forecasting program and prediction-powered inference ([Lineage](/start-here/lineage/#outside-precedents-for-the-core-pattern)). This is not the AI-model "evals" field, though the two overlap wherever an LLM is the judge ([Chapter 1](/start-here/introduction/#not-ai-model-evals-though-they-overlap)).

## The shape of the book

- **The systems view.** Why a system is a different object from an evaluation, and the properties that define its design space: accuracy, unit cost, robustness to gaming, legitimacy. [Evaluation as a System](/concepts/the-systems-view/), [The Four Components](/concepts/components/).
- **Methods and techniques.** Ways to produce evaluations, ways to tie cheap and expensive judgment together, what LLMs change, how to measure an evaluator, and how to grade and launch a system. [Evaluation Methods](/concepts/evaluation-methods/), [Techniques](/concepts/techniques/), [LLMs as Evaluators](/concepts/llm-evaluators/), [Measuring an Evaluator](/concepts/measuring-an-evaluator/), [a worked example](/concepts/worked-example/) with numbers, the [Capability Ladder](/concepts/capability-ladder/) and a [Launch Checklist](/concepts/launch-checklist/).
- **Evidence.** About 160 standing evaluation systems, and what the literature establishes across them: reactivity, Goodhart effects, certification economics, rating inflation. [Evaluation Systems in the Wild](/concepts/evaluation-systems-in-the-wild/), [Patterns & Failure Modes](/concepts/patterns-and-failure-modes/).
- **The environment.** What happens when the evaluated push back. [Epistemic Culture](/concepts/epistemic-culture/).

The open questions are collected as [Cruxes](/start-here/key-questions/) and [Open Problems](/open-questions/). The whole wiki is also available as one file, [llms-full.txt](/llms-full.txt).
